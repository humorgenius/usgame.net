The GTA series has always been known for adult-oriented content, and GTA6 carries that tradition forward. Rating information for North America, Europe and Australia has gradually come into focus, and the overall positioning is still that of a title meant for adult players only.

## North America (ESRB): Mature 17+

In May 2026, the ESRB M (Mature 17+) mark for GTA6 appeared in an official Sony PlayStation marketing email, together with the full list of content descriptors:

- Blood and Gore
- Intense Violence
- Mature Humor
- Nudity
- Strong Language
- Strong Sexual Content
- Use of Drugs and Alcohol

This matches GTA5's rating exactly. Although Rockstar's official site still showed “Rating Pending” at times, the explicit mark in the platform's email has been treated by the industry as substantive confirmation.

An M rating means the game is intended for players 17 and older and contains violence, sexual suggestiveness, profanity and drug use that may upset minors. Retailers typically use it to restrict sales to minors.

![North America (ESRB): Mature 17+](/media/52a1ee26b494.webp)

## Europe and the UK (PEGI): PEGI 18 Expected

As of now, PEGI's official database has not published a final result, but the industry widely expects PEGI 18.

The reason is simple: earlier titles in the series (GTA5, Red Dead Redemption 2 and others) are all rated PEGI 18, and GTA6 matches or even exceeds them in its level of violence, sexual content and language. PEGI 18 is the highest rating and is recommended for players 18 and over only.

Major markets such as the UK, Germany and France will enforce that standard strictly, with age verification in both physical and digital stores.

![Europe and the UK (PEGI): PEGI 18 Expected](/media/69bf0ba82d69.webp)

## Australia (ACB): MA 15+

Australia's Classification Board issued an MA 15+ rating in 2025, with consumer advice that includes:

- Strong Violence
- Strong Sexual References
- Sexualised Imagery
- Strong Nudity
- Strong Sex Scenes
- Strong Coarse Language
- Strong Crude Humour

MA 15+ allows purchase by players 15 and over, and those under 18 are expected to play with parental guidance. That rating is slightly lower than GTA5's R18+ in Australia, but it still belongs to the restricted category.

## Why the Content Earns a High Rating

Judging by the trailers and the Extended Look released so far, the level of content in GTA6 lines up closely with the series' established style:

- Violence: gunfights, melee combat, car crashes and police chases show clear blood and injury.
- Sexual content: strip clubs, character interactions and some missions and NPC dialogue include explicit sexual suggestiveness and nudity.
- Language: profanity and adult humour run through the dialogue and the radio stations.
- Drugs and alcohol: both the story and the open world include scenes of drug use and drinking.

Rockstar has not tried to lower the rating by cutting content; it has kept the series' signature adult satire and dark humour.

## What It Means for Players and Parents

- Adult players: the rating does not affect purchase or play, and the content matches what the series has led people to expect.
- Minors: strongly not recommended. Even if age verification can technically be bypassed, the violence, sexual content and language are not suitable for minors.
- Parents: use the console's built-in family management features to set age limits and spending controls.

Online mode has not launched, so the rating currently covers single-player content. Once multiplayer arrives, notices such as “Online Interactions Not Rated” may be added.

## Summary

GTA6 is firmly positioned as an adult title: ESRB Mature 17+ in North America, PEGI 18 expected in Europe, and MA 15+ in Australia. Its content descriptors cover bloody violence, nudity, strong sexual content, profanity and drug use, all closely in line with the series' tradition.

For the intended audience the rating comes as no surprise; for parents hoping to expose minors to the game, it is a clear warning signal. The game launches on November 19, 2026, and the final, complete rating information will be published by the ratings boards when it goes on sale.
