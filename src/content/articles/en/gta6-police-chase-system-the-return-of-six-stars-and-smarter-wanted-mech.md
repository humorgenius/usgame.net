GTA6's police chase system is one of the biggest upgrades the series has seen. In the Extended Look, Rockstar clearly showed a six-star wanted level and introduced tracking logic built on a “description” rather than position alone. Crimes no longer trigger an alert automatically: police narrow in on a suspect step by step using witness sightings, surveillance, clothing and vehicles, turning an escape from “floor it and hide in a corner” into a genuinely tactical exercise.

## The Six-Star Wanted Level Returns

GTA5 capped the wanted level at five stars, while GTA6 restores the classic six-star system. In the chase shown in the Extended Look, two slots sit empty at four stars, confirming a maximum of six.

Six stars means a higher tier of law enforcement response, potentially including heavier firepower, more helicopters and even military units (the exact response has not been fully detailed). But the real change is not the number of stars, it is the logic underneath.

## A Crime Has to Be Reported

Rockstar North co-head Rob Nelson said plainly:

“GTA6's wanted system requires that someone witnesses the crime, or that an alarm is triggered, before it is reported to the police.”

This means:

- A crime with no witnesses, no cameras and no alarms may not generate a wanted level right away.
- Bystanders, shop clerks and CCTV footage become key variables.
- Players can cut the risk by removing witnesses, disabling cameras or getting clear of the scene fast.

The design sits closer to the witness logic of Red Dead Redemption 2 than to the “crime equals wanted level” approach of earlier entries.

![A Crime Has to Be Reported](/media/b09bf943b26f.webp)

## Heat Icons: What Do the Police Know?

A row of heat icons now sits beneath the wanted stars, showing in real time what the police have on you:

| **Icon Type** | **Meaning** | **Counter** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Figure silhouette | Police have a description of your appearance | Wear a mask, change hairstyle, add hat/glasses |
| Coat hanger | Police know your current clothing | Change your entire outfit |
| Vehicle | Police know the vehicle you are using | Swap, ditch or burn the car |
| Two-person silhouette | Police know a pair committed the crime | Split up |
| Camera | You were caught on surveillance | Destroy the relevant cameras or evidence |
| Weapon | Police know the type of weapon used | Drop or swap the weapon |

The more icons there are, the more easily the police can identify and pursue you. Players can erase that information one piece at a time with targeted actions.

## How the Stars Change

The wanted stars themselves change appearance depending on the situation:

- Hollow star: police know a crime happened but have no specific description.
- Solid white star: police have identified you and have direct line of sight.
- Flashing or red star: they have lost direct sight but are still searching the area.

This makes it clearer whether you are being chased or being searched for.

## Escape Tactics Get a Full Overhaul

The old move of ducking into an alley and waiting for the stars to vanish no longer cuts it. Effective methods include:

## 1. Change clothes and disguise

Clothing is a key identifying factor. A car trunk can hold spare outfits, and a quick change clears the “coat hanger” icon.

## 2. Switch vehicles

Abandon the flagged car and take a new one to clear the vehicle icon. Torching the old car is also a common practice.

## 3. Split up

Pulling jobs together as both protagonists triggers a “pair” icon. Separating can lower the wanted level or split police attention.

## 4. Leave the hot zone

A crime scene creates a “hot zone,” and going back can re-trigger the wanted level. Heat fades gradually once you move away.

## 5. Surrender option at a low wanted level

At a low star level a surrender option may appear, letting you avoid a straight firefight.

![5. Surrender option at a low wanted level](/media/1af735d50923.webp)

## Police AI and Response Feel More Real

- Police no longer spawn out of thin air next to the player; they are dispatched from nearby and actually drive to the scene.
- The minimap hides police positions by default, so you need the “Focus” ability to sense nearby law enforcement.
- Carrying a gun in the open immediately draws the attention of bystanders and police, raising the risk further.

## Compared with Earlier Games

| **Item** | **GTA5** | **GTA6 (Confirmed)** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maximum wanted level | 5 stars | 6 stars |
| How crimes trigger it | Mostly automatic | Requires a witness or an alarm |
| Identification basis | Mainly position | Appearance, clothing, vehicle, surveillance, whether you are in a pair |
| Escape priority | Break line of sight | Clear description data + break line of sight |
| Police on the minimap | Usually visible | Hidden by default, must be sensed |

## What the Design Means

The new system turns crime and escape from a simple chase into a game of information and disguise. Players have to think like a real fugitive: Did anyone see me? Are there cameras? Have my clothes and my car already been logged? Does moving as a pair raise the risk?

It forms a tight loop with the clothing system, the dual-protagonist switching and the NPC reaction system, giving every crime more weight and more strategic depth.

![What the Design Means](/media/a2979307cfa5.webp)

## Summary

GTA6's police chase system is built around the return of six stars, witness-triggered alerts, description-based tracking and heat icons, and it fundamentally changes the series' classic escape experience. Players no longer just need to run fast enough and hide long enough; they have to actively manage their own “criminal file,” chipping away at their heat by changing clothes, swapping cars, splitting up and wiping out evidence.

After launch, every shootout, every robbery and every high-wanted-level chase across Leonida will become a survival game that genuinely tests the player's wits and reflexes. On November 19, 2026, players will get hands-on with the smartest, most oppressive police system the series has ever had.
