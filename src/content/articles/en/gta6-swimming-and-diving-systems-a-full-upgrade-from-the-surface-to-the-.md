Water and underwater gameplay in the GTA series has gone through several iterations. From San Andreas introducing swimming for the first time to GTA5 adding a full diving kit and seabed exploration, GTA6 pushes the system further across the Florida-inspired state of Leonida. Below is a rundown of the swimming and diving content confirmed so far, based on official promotional images, the Extended Look and reliable reports.

## Officially Confirmed Diving Activities

In its official website introduction to the Leonida Keys, Rockstar published a promotional image of a diver swimming through a coral reef alongside sea turtles and eels. That directly confirms scuba diving exists as a playable activity.

Diving is described as optional but required for 100% completion. Players can explore:

- Coral reefs
- Shipwrecks
- Underwater caves
- Hidden loot

Players must also manage their oxygen supply and face underwater hazards. Some content also supports photographing marine life.

## Swimming System Basics

Standard swimming returns and has been improved. Players can swim freely on the surface, and an oxygen bar appears once you enter the water. As in GTA5, a lung capacity attribute determines how long you can stay underwater without gear, and it can be raised through practice.

From the footage released so far, swimming animations are smoother and the transitions between surface and underwater are natural. Characters recover their posture quickly after hitting the water, avoiding the occasional stiffness of earlier titles.

![Swimming System Basics](/media/18b325774211.webp)

## Oxygen and Equipment Mechanics

While diving, oxygen management becomes central:

- Scuba gear greatly extends underwater time.
- Without gear you rely on lung capacity, and health starts draining once oxygen runs out.
- Depth limits still apply, and going too deep can cause extra damage (similar to GTA5's pressure mechanics).

Equipment details are already visible in the promotional images, including realistic components such as a regulator and a buoyancy compensator, showing Rockstar's push for greater visual realism. Players may need to equip gear manually or prepare a dive set from a boat, rather than having it appear automatically as in previous games.

## Underwater World and Ecology

The tropical waters of the Leonida Keys provide an ideal diving backdrop. Official images have shown:

- Coral reef environments
- Marine life including sea turtles and eels
- Richer underwater vegetation and terrain

Combined with the “Underwater Areas” confirmed on the map, the sea is no longer just a decorative boundary but a complete explorable space. Dangerous creatures such as sharks are expected to return, adding tension. Shipwrecks and caves provide objectives for collection and missions.

![Underwater World and Ecology](/media/4dc0aa6381d6.webp)

## Synergy with Related Water Activities

Swimming and diving aren't isolated systems; they complement other water-based gameplay:

- Kayaking: suited to shallow water and swamp areas.
- Airboat: designed specifically for the Grassrivers wetlands.
- Jet Ski: high-speed cruising along the coast.
- Regular boats: for longer-distance water travel.

Together these activities turn Leonida's waters from an “obstacle” into a “gameplay stage.”

## Design Significance

Florida's geography (island chains, shallows and the handoff to deep sea) gives the underwater system a natural stage. Rockstar has elevated diving from a GTA5 “optional extra” to a formal activity tied to completion, while keeping tension through oxygen management and dangerous creatures so the underwater world never becomes a risk-free sightseeing zone.

This fits the broader design direction of “more real, more consequential”: entering the water is no longer a simple shortcut for dodging police but an action that requires preparation and planning.

## Summary

GTA6's swimming and diving systems are confirmed to return and to be significantly upgraded. Regular swimming covers basic water traversal, while scuba diving unlocks deeper exploration such as coral reefs, shipwrecks and caves. Oxygen management and marine ecology add strategy and immersion. The Leonida Keys serves as the core stage for the system.

After launch, players will be able to dive into Leonida's tropical waters for real and experience the most complete underwater world the series has delivered. Whether for 100% completion, hidden rewards or simply taking in the underwater scenery, swimming and diving will be an indispensable part of exploring this Sunshine State.
